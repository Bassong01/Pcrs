METHODOLOGYThe analysis document that adheres to a specific method for the project explains the rationale behind this choice. All of the elements presented in this document contribute to a clearer understanding of the information system being modeled, offering a structured view of the issue and the proposed solution, supported by diagrams.Choosing an appropriate methodology is a critical step in the analysis and design phase, as it determines how the system's requirements, structure, and behavior will be captured and represented. Two methodologies were considered for this project: MERISE and UML, presented and compared below.a. Comparative Study Between MERISE and UMLBefore selecting an analysis approach for the Police Criminal Record System, two major methodologies were considered: MERISE and UML.MERISE is a systems analysis and design method developed in France in the 1970s, structured around a strict separation of data and processing, modeled respectively through the Conceptual Data Model (CDM) and the Conceptual Processing Model (CPM). It follows a sequential, top-down life cycle and is well suited to information systems with a strong data-processing orientation, but it offers limited support for object-oriented design and for modeling dynamic, interactive behavior.UML (Unified Modeling Language), on the other hand, is an object-oriented modeling language that unifies the representation of a system's structure and behavior through a coherent set of diagrams (use case, class, sequence, activity, among others). It supports iterative and incremental development, facilitates the modeling of complex interactions between actors and the system, and is the de facto standard in modern software engineering, particularly for web-based and object-oriented systems.The table below summarizes the main differences between the two approaches:CriteriaMERISEUMLOriginFrance, 1970sObject Management Group (OMG)ParadigmStructured / data-orientedObject-orientedData and processingModeled separatelyModeled together (classes)DiagramsCDM, CPM, MCT, etc.Use Case, Class, Sequence, Activity, etc.Development approachSequentialIterative and incrementalSuitability for web systemsLimitedWell suitedGiven the object-oriented nature of the Police Criminal Record System and the need to model interactions between multiple actors (administrators, officers, judicial authorities) and system behavior in detail, UML was retained as the modeling language for this project.b. Presentation of the 2TUPHaving retained UML as the modeling language, a specific development process was needed to organize how the models would be produced and articulated throughout the project. The 2TUP (Two Track Unified Process) was selected for this purpose.The 2TUP is a variant of the Unified Process (UP) proposed by Valtech, designed to address the frequent and continuous changes affecting information systems, whether they originate from evolving functional requirements or from changes in technical environments (networks, hardware, operating systems, standards). To manage these two types of constraints independently before merging them, the 2TUP is structured according to a "Y" shape, built around three branches:•	The functional branch, which captures, analyzes, and models the business requirements of the system independently of any technical consideration. This branch produces the use case, sequence, and activity diagrams;•	The technical branch, which captures and models the technical constraints and choices of the target environment (architecture, development tools, technology stack), independently of the business logic;•	The realization branch, which merges the results of the functional and technical branches to produce the actual system design (class diagram, database design, and overall architecture), leading to the coding, testing, and delivery of the system.This dual-track structure allows functional and technical work to progress in parallel, reducing the impact of changes in either dimension on the overall project and resulting in a system design that is both aligned with user needs and adapted to the technical environment./Figure 3: The "Y" shape of the 2TUP development processc. Characteristics of the 2TUPAs a variant of the Unified Process, the 2TUP inherits and reinforces several key characteristics that shaped how the Police Criminal Record System was analyzed and designed:•	Use-case driven: the development process is guided by the use cases, which express the functional needs of the actors and serve as the thread running through the entire analysis, from requirements capture to testing;•	Architecture-centrist: technical constraints are treated as first-class citizens from the earliest stages, ensuring that the system's architecture is defined in parallel with, and not merely after, the functional requirements;•	Iterative and incremental: the system is built through successive iterations, each producing a more refined and complete version of the models and, eventually, of the system itself, allowing requirements to be adjusted as understanding deepens;•	Risk-driven: by separating the functional and technical branches, the 2TUP allows risks specific to each dimension (e.g., misunderstood business rules on one side, unsuitable technology choices on the other) to be identified and addressed early and independently;•	Component-based: the resulting system is structured into reusable, loosely coupled components, facilitating maintenance and future evolution of the system;•	Trace-ability: each artifact produced (use cases, diagrams, code) can be traced back to the requirement that motivated it, ensuring that the final system remains aligned with the initial needs expressed in the specification book.These characteristics make the 2TUP particularly well suited to a project such as the Police Criminal Record System, where both the functional requirements (roles, workflows, data confidentiality) and the technical environment (limited infrastructure, network constraints across SOFTINFRA TECH's regional and local services) needed to be addressed with equal rigor.d. Justification of the Choice of the AnalysisThe choice of UML combined with the 2TUP process for the analysis and design of the Police Criminal Record System is justified by several factors specific to the context of this project.First, the system involves multiple actors with distinct roles and responsibilities (administrators, police officers, judicial authorities, and other SOFTINFRA TECH personnel), whose interactions with the system are best captured through use case and sequence diagrams rather than the more data-centric models offered by MERISE.Second, the project had to reconcile functional requirements, expressed through interviews and the specification book, with technical constraints inherent to the SOFTINFRA TECH's environment, notably limited infrastructure and uneven connectivity across regional and local services. The 2TUP's separation of the functional and technical branches allowed these two dimensions to be addressed independently before being merged into a coherent design, reducing the risk of a solution that is functionally sound but technically unrealistic, or vice versa.Third, given the internship's limited time frame, an iterative and incremental approach allowed the models to be progressively refined as understanding of the SOFTINFRA TECH's processes deepened, rather than requiring a complete and definitive specification from the outset.For these reasons, the combination of UML and the 2TUP was retained as the most appropriate approach for the analysis and design of the system, providing both the expressive power needed to model complex actor interactions and the flexibility needed to accommodate the project's technical and organizational constraints.II.
